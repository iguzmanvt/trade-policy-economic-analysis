# Mexico's FDI: financing composition and the sources of change

**Isaac Guzmán Valdivia Tovar · Independent economic analysis · 13 November 2026**

The largest component of foreign direct investment (FDI) need not be the component driving its change. In the preserved second-quarter 2026 statistical edition, reinvested earnings account for **67.0% of Mexico's 2025 FDI**, yet decline relative to 2024. Total FDI rises **10.40%**, with arithmetic contributions of **7.55 percentage points from new investments**, **−1.65 points from reinvested earnings** and **4.50 points from intercompany accounts**.

This sample reconstructs national financing composition from **84 published figures** of Mexico's Ministry of Economy / National Registry of Foreign Investments: 80 figures for revised annual totals in 2006–2025, plus four figures for the originally reported January–June 2026 total. The partial-year snapshot remains separate. It records USD 34,967.5 million, with reinvested earnings accounting for 88.5%; this share is not treated as directly comparable evidence of structural change against full-year composition.

The categories identify financing flows, not their physical use. New investments can include capital contributions and ownership transfers; they are not synonymous with greenfield projects or announcements. Neither financing composition nor its arithmetic decomposition establishes employment, productive capacity or a causal policy effect.

All values are current USD millions. Shares use the published national total, and annual growth contributions use the previous year's published total. Exact residuals are preserved rather than allocated. No growth rate compares a half-year observation with a full-year total, and no confidential state-level figures are included or inferred.

The package includes two figures, source-cell locators, pinned small inputs, standard-library Python calculations and an independent rational-arithmetic auditor. Current validation covers the archived extract and reproducibility. Official-workbook recovery was recorded on 5 November 2026 and was not repeated for this preparation. This is a fixed historical edition, not a live update service.
