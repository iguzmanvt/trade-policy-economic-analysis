# Two-line project descriptions

## Observed duty intensity and trade reallocation

Built a reproducible comparison of observed U.S. import duty intensity across three tariff lines, five suppliers and two January–August periods.

Distinguished supplier-specific duty gaps from statutory tariffs and causal claims about Mexico's trade expansion.

## Trade concentration and exposure: partners and products

Measured all-goods U.S. supplier and destination exposure for January–August 2023–2026 and concentration within a fixed import basket from Mexico for 2025–2026.

Reproduced concentration indices and signed growth contributions with separate partner/product denominators, archived vintages and a bridge between general and consumption imports.

## Non-tariff barriers in National Trade Estimate reports

Compared eight selected market-access issues across the 2024–2026 National Trade Estimate editions, distinguishing reported concerns, access safeguards and changes in reporting.

Built a 24-cell panel with 19 positive evidence records and five chapter-reviewed non-mentions, preserving source-specific locators and tracing institutional changes over time.

## Mexico input–output linkages

Reconstructed Mexico's official dairy-product use accounts, distinguishing 41.5% imported origin in intermediate uses from 17.5% in all gross uses and identifying destination groups accounting for 74.3% of intermediate demand.

Recovered six exact official workbook binaries, preserved the 2018 extract and four tables, and strengthened validation with checks active under optimized Python and a reproducible note and figure.

## Growth and policy scenarios

Developed a reproducible Solow-model scenario exercise extending my public economic-growth teaching materials.

Compared investment and efficiency-growth paths, separating transition costs, long-run mechanisms and illustrative assumptions from empirical forecasts.
